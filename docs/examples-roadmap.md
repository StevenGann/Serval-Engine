# Examples roadmap

Examples drive the engine: each one is written as a game developer would write it, and what it needs (or has to work around) becomes engine API. This is the list of candidates, chosen to show the engine's strengths, teach its use, and expose its weaknesses. The existing examples are in [`examples/`](../examples/) ([getting-started.md](getting-started.md#1-build-the-examples) has a reading order).

**Status:** implemented: `platformer` ([what it exposed](#what-platformer-exposed)), `breakout` ([below](#what-breakout-exposed)), `shmup` ([below](#what-shmup-exposed)), `blackjack` ([below](#what-blackjack-exposed)) `fireflies` ([below](#what-fireflies-exposed)), `raycaster` ([below](#what-raycaster-exposed)), `racer` ([below](#what-racer-exposed)), `rhythm` ([below](#what-rhythm-exposed)) `boardgames` ([below](#what-boardgames-showed)) and `den` ([below](#what-den-showed)), and "Save and high scores" in `asteroids` ([`scores.c`](../examples/asteroids/scores.c); [below](#what-the-high-score-table-exposed)), next to `hello`, `bunnymark` and `pong`; and `effects`, a scene per screen effect as each is implemented (so far alpha blending, [`blend_fx.c`](../examples/effects/blend_fx.c), runtime sprite tiles, [`sprite_tiles_fx.c`](../examples/effects/sprite_tiles_fx.c), and raster effects, [`raster_fx.c`](../examples/effects/raster_fx.c)). Everything else on this page is a candidate. ✱ marks examples blocked on engine features that are still only designs or planned API. Most gaps the implemented examples exposed are closed by engine API; the ones still open are gathered in [Open gaps](#open-gaps). The 1.0 API freeze ([api-freeze.md](api-freeze.md)) closed some and declared others as *planned* API, named where they appear: a declared gap stays open until a 1.x version implements it.

Each entry says what the example **shows** and what it would **expose**: missing API, limits, or workarounds that should become engine features.

## Open gaps

Every gap still open from the sections below, in one place, with the planned API the freeze declared for it (closed ones are struck through there, and here where the freeze closed them). API surprises that aren't missing features are in [open-questions.md](open-questions.md#api-caveats-found-by-the-examples).

- **Sprites:** ~~writing a sprite's tiles at runtime, like `tileset_set_tiles` (composed card faces)~~ (closed by `sprite_set_tiles()`, declared in 1.0 and implemented since: [sprites.md](sprites.md#runtime-tiles)); the 32-matrix limit with many distinct angles.
- **Palettes and effects:** ~~palette writes (a shadow palette)~~ (closed by `sprite_set_colors()` and `tileset_set_colors()`, declared in 1.0 and implemented since: [sprites.md](sprites.md#palettes), [tilemaps.md](tilemaps.md#palette-writes)), with palette cycling and fades as game code over them and `color_mix()` ([`effects`](../examples/effects/palette_fx.c) shows both); ~~alpha blending~~ (closed by `screen_set_blend()` and `SPRITE_BLEND`, declared in 1.0 and implemented since, pausing while `screen_set_brightness()` is not 0, since the hardware has one color effect: [runtime-systems.md](runtime-systems.md#alpha-blending); `effects` shows it); windows and mosaic (later, no API yet).
- **Text and UI:** a variable-width or large font (also for sprites); text above every sprite but `SPRITE_ABOVE_HUD`, on an 8-pixel grid; a text-input or menu helper; dialogue boxes.
- **Motion:** tweens, easing and springs; swept collision and body-to-body response; ~~slopes and ladders~~ (implemented: `MAP_SLOPE_*`, `MAP_LADDER` and `MAP_CONTACT_LADDER`, [tilemaps.md](tilemaps.md#floor-slopes); `platformer`'s stage 1-1 has a 45° hill, a 1:2 ramp and a ladder to a ledge); a body placed overlapping a solid metatile falls through it (by design).
- **Maps:** `MAP_MAX_CHANGES` (64 runtime cell changes); tileset groups, to load tiles several rooms share once (later, no API yet: [tilemaps.md](tilemaps.md#tilesets); the platformer's stages each repeat the block tiles, [below](#the-four-stages)). ~~Map bodies bounce at most 255/256 of their speed~~: closed, `body_bounce` 255 is a perfect bounce ([tilemaps.md](tilemaps.md#map-bodies)).
- **Diagnostics:** a plain (non-double) affine mode for small angles (shrunk and mirrored sprites already use it). (What the hardware limits drop, including the per-scanline budget, is counted by `sprite_stats()`.)
- **Sound:** tracker music and sampled sound effects: implemented through Maxmod (`audio_bank_set()`, `music_*()`, `sfx_*()`; the `jukebox` example), on the GBA and on the web. Scripts play them through SYS calls 17 to 30 ([vm.md](vm.md#engine-calls)). ~~No way to ask the tracker where it is~~ (closed by `music_position()` and `music_ticks()`, SYS 53 for scripts, added for [`rhythm`](#what-rhythm-exposed): [audio.md](audio.md#position)). The web's audio latency is known to the page's JavaScript but not to games, which calibrate by hand ([open-questions.md](open-questions.md); [`rhythm`](#what-rhythm-exposed)). (Wave-channel music is implemented too: `blackjack`'s walking bass plays on it.) No way to change a playing PSG tone's pitch, or to stop one channel without ending a music pause ([`racer`](#what-racer-exposed)'s engine drone).
- **Hardware and input:** HBlank / per-scanline effects: implemented, one at a time, as `raster_scroll()`, `raster_backdrop()` and `raster_clear()` ([runtime-systems.md](runtime-systems.md#raster-effects)); one at a time carries a road but not hills, nor a raster sky beside it ([`racer`](#what-racer-exposed)); input recording and playback; input remapping; a touch API; input timed within a frame (a press is known to the frame that polls it, ±0.5 frame: [`rhythm`](#what-rhythm-exposed)).
- **ECS and VRAM:** only 15 game component bits; components added and removed by writing `ent_mask`, which stays legal (helpers may come later as wrappers); ~~all-or-nothing sprite residency~~ (closed by `sprite_groups_mark()` and `sprite_groups_release()`: global and per-room groups; and by streamed groups, `SPRITE_GROUP_STREAMED`), no defragmentation.
- **Pacing (from [`boardgames`](#what-boardgames-showed)):** a game that spends whatever a frame has left (a search) can only guess it from the last frame: nothing says how many cycles the current frame has used so far; a mid-frame cycle query (the `frame_cpu_cycles()` of the picture so far) would let it stop at the budget instead of aiming under it. Not planned; an observation.
- **Projects:** a shared header of sound IDs, as a generated project would have; levels as const cells in ROM (a Studio Advance build gives that; nothing in the engine to change).
- **Scripting (VM):** aiming a path from a script. ~~The scheduler's fixed cost per frame~~: cut 2026-10-10, from about 7,000 cycles a frame with nothing to run to about 620 (the scheduler visits only the contexts in use and the bound entities, [vm.md](vm.md#implementation-notes); measured by the [script benchmark](development.md#script-benchmark)). ~~Collision pairs still in C~~: closed by `vm_collide()`, the VM's own collision pass ([vm.md](vm.md#collisions), [below](#what-fireflies-exposed)). (The VM revision, milestone 6 of [vm.md](vm.md#milestones), closed per-instance variables, iterating an object's instances, arrays, game components and animation timers set from scripts, a field width for `text_print_number`, and `path_stop`.)
- **3D (the pixel surface):** from [`raycaster`](#what-raycaster-exposed): a sine finer than 1/256; a fast division or reciprocal in `math.h`; a word copy games' IWRAM code can call (the compiler's `memcpy()` is in ROM); text over the surface without a font of the game's own, composed into sprite frames; per-note volume and duty in PSG music, and jingles on more than one channel; reading the cycle counter in mid-frame, to time a part of a frame; pacing a picture over several frames. ~~No way to draw pixels at all~~ (closed by the pixel surface, `surface.h`: [surface.md](surface.md)).

## Order

Done, in the order they were written:

1. **`platformer`**: the biggest gap between Serval and a typical engine was backgrounds, scrolling and map collision, and a first level in the classic style needs all three.
2. **Save and high scores** (in `asteroids`; later also `breakout`, `shmup`, `blackjack`, `servle`, the last from a Lua script, and `racer`): `save.h`.
3. **`breakout`**: pushed for directional contacts and `ecs_count`.
4. **`shmup`** (written alongside `breakout`): screen-space entities, fixed and self-scrolling layers, paths, cheap per-kind loops.
5. **`blackjack`**: per-draw palettes, and the sprite gaps (metasprites, scaling, runtime tiles) that a card game or poker roguelike runs into.
6. **`fireflies`**: the bytecode VM's first whole game; what scripts can't yet say, and the VM's first measured costs.
7. **`raycaster`**: the first 3D example, written to find out what the engine must provide for 3D (the pixel surface) and what it should leave to the game (everything else).
8. **`racer`**: the first game on raster effects, written to find out how far one effect at a time carries a game.
9. **`rhythm`**: the first game timed by its music, written to find out what a game needs to keep time with tracker music (the music's position).
10. **`boardgames`**: the first game whose work is a search, written to find out what the GBA's CPU does with one: how far an engine-free C core, a search split across frames and fixed pools go (no engine change).
11. **`den`**: the first game whose whole premise is the cartridge's clock, written to find out what a game that lives while it is off needs from `rtc.h` and from itself (no engine change).

Next:

1. **Sprite stress test**: turns hidden limits into documented numbers and tests both renderers.
2. **Tutorial series**: the biggest win for newcomers, kept honest by CI building it.

The dungeon crawler and the match-three puzzle (under [Genre games](#genre-games)) are candidates after those, in no fixed order.

## What `platformer` exposed

Written against `map.h` as a game developer would; everything it needed beyond that was worked around in the example and listed here as candidates for engine API. Most are now closed, and the example uses the new API instead of its workarounds:

- ~~**No animation timing.**~~ Closed by `sys_animate` with `C_ANIM`, `spr_anim_time` and `SpriteAsset.frame_times` ([ecs.h](api-reference.md#ecsh)): beetles walk, gems spin, sparkles twinkle and debris tumbles by themselves. The gem's mirrored turning frame and the debris' three flipped frames are `frame_order` steps with `SPRITE_FRAME_FLIP_*` instead of tiles of their own (7 tiles less). The serval's frame is still picked by the game (it depends on what the serval does), and blinking uses `SPRITE_HIDDEN`.
- ~~**No fade API, no animated tiles, an empty frame after `map_load()`.**~~ Closed by `screen_set_brightness` (fades between title, stage card, level, game over and stage clear), `tileset_set_tiles` (a glint sweeps across the bonus blocks) and `map_load` drawing at once. Palette cycling needed palette writes, then planned API and since implemented as `tileset_set_colors()`; animated tiles cover the bonus block.
- ~~**Text has one color and no background.**~~ Closed by `text_set_color` and `text_set_shadow`: the HUD has a drop shadow, so the clouds are back up behind it. `text_print_centered` replaced the example's own helper. Enemies walking on the highest bricks still pass behind the HUD row (a HUD band would avoid it).
- ~~**Sound: no music, no priority, the silent slide.**~~ Closed by music (`PsgSong`, `psg_music_play`), `PsgSound.priority` (jingles aren't cut short by a gem chime; screen changes stop only the music with `psg_music_stop`, so the start chime plays on) and `psg_play`'s warning for an upward slide that runs past the highest pitch. The rest closed by `psg_music_pause`/`psg_music_resume` (the example pauses the tune with the game and resumes it where it stopped) and `psg_music_set_tempo` (the hurry-up speeds the tune up from where it is).
- ~~**Gravity for things that aren't map bodies, no maximum fall speed.**~~ Closed by map bodies bouncing and sliding (`body_bounce`, `body_friction` in `sys_map_movement`) and `body_max_fall`: popping gems, the fish and knocked-out enemies are map bodies, the serval's terminal speed is `body_max_fall`, and brick debris falls through the level under `sys_physics` with every edge of its bounds open. The extra gravity for short hops stays game logic.
- ~~**Bodies against bodies.**~~ Closed by `body_hit_side`: a stomp is `body_hit_side(player, enemy) == BODY_SIDE_BOTTOM`. Bodies that already overlapped before the frame get `BODY_SIDE_INSIDE`, never a guessed side (it used to be the side of least overlap, which could report a paddle's face to a ball that had slipped past it). Pong keeps its own paddle test: it catches a ball that was in front of the face last frame even when the ball clips the paddle's end first, which `body_hit_side` reports as `BODY_SIDE_TOP`/`BOTTOM`.
- **A body placed overlapping a solid metatile falls through it** (by design, so bodies can leave walls). A fish rising out of a block still has to be snapped exactly on top before becoming a map body.
- ~~**Randomness and replays.**~~ Closed: `random_entropy()` now depends only on the input history and `frame_count()`, so the same input plays the same game on the GBA and the web. The frogs hop at random intervals again, seeded when START is pressed.
- **Levels are text converted when a stage starts** into one buffer of cells in EWRAM, sized for the largest stage. A game made in Studio Advance would get const cells in ROM from its build; nothing in the engine needs to change for that. `map_load()` keeps a pointer to the `MapLayer`, so a game that builds its layers at run time keeps them static and refills them only while they are unloaded (here on the black stage card).

### The four stages

The single level became stages: a frame (`game.c`, `level.c`, `player.c`, `objects.c`) plays a table of `StageDef`s, each stage a module of its own files (`stage_<name>.c` with its level text and hooks, `art_<name>.c`, `sound_<name>.c`). Stage 1-1 moved in unchanged (the same input gives the same frames, but for the HUD row), and 1-2, the underground, is new. What it used and exposed:

- **Sprite memory per stage: closed by marks.** The sprites every stage uses form a global group loaded once, then `sprite_groups_mark()`; each stage's group (its enemies, and the bouncing block and debris in its own colors) loads above the mark and is released to it with `sprite_groups_release()` on the next stage card. The one sprite table holds every ID, in a block per stage; groups load subsets. The block and debris are listed in every stage's group (never two loaded at once), so a stage's bricks can bounce in its own colors with a palette slot of its own group.
- **One-way platforms and hazards: worked as designed.** Plank ledges are `MAP_ONEWAY`; enemies walk on them too. Freeing a tag for hazards meant telling the fish's bonus block apart by its metatile number instead of a tag; crystal spikes are solid metatiles tagged `TAG_HAZARD`, found with `map_tags_in()` around the serval's body (and the row under its feet). Bonus blocks and gems are found with `map_tags_in()` too.
- **Palette effects waited on palette writes.** `tileset_set_colors()` was planned API when this was written (it is implemented now, and the example keeps its workarounds), so the underground's colors couldn't change while shown: its dim parallax layer is the near rock's palette mixed toward the backdrop with `color_mix()` when the stage loads, into RAM palettes its `Tileset` points at, and its pulsing glow is the backdrop (`screen_set_backdrop()` with `color_mix()`). Glowing crystals or a lava glow on the tiles themselves need palette writes.
- **No tileset groups.** Every stage's tileset carries its own copy of the shared bonus block, used block, gem and brick tiles, and the bonus block's glint has to be told which tile number it starts at in each (`StageDef.bonus_tile`). Tileset groups (later, no API yet: [tilemaps.md](tilemaps.md#tilesets)) would load the shared tiles once.
- **Paths on enemies.** Bats swoop on a `path.h` path through the rock (bodies with `BODY_GRAVITY(0)`, moved by `sys_movement`). Stomping one must end its path with `path_stop()`: `C_PATH` may not be removed by hand. A caveat, not a gap.
- **Flash saves.** The first example on `SAVE FLASH64K`: one versioned struct (furthest stage, best score) in slot 0, written only on black screens since a Flash write takes a few frames (mGBA detects the Flash at the first read, at boot). The web build keeps the same bytes in `localStorage`.
- **Slopes and a ladder: worked as designed.** Stage 1-1 has a 45° hill and a hill of 1:2 slopes (`MAP_SLOPE_*`, drawn to the shapes [tilemaps.md](tilemaps.md#floor-slopes) gives them, with no grass on the ground metatile under a slope) and a ladder up to a stone ledge (`MAP_LADDER`). The serval and the beetles walk them with no code of their own: `sys_map_movement` keeps a body's foot on the slope and makes the ladder's top a floor. Climbing is the game's, as the engine leaves it (`player.c`): Up with `MAP_CONTACT_LADDER` and a ladder at the serval's feet (`map_collision_at()`), or Down standing on the ladder's top, takes its gravity away and lines it up with the ladder; it stops when the contact ends (over the top it lands on the top), at the bottom, or with a jump. The serval has no climbing frame, so it climbs in its jump pose, turning from side to side. No engine gaps.
- **Room for more:** the sound table keeps every stage's effects at IDs reserved per stage (`SND_STAGE()`), rather than a table per stage; either works with `psg_table_set()`, which stops effects but not music. A stage's own components share the six game bits left (`C_STAGE(0)` to `C_STAGE(5)`), since only one stage plays at a time.

#### Stage 1-3, the treetops

High in a forest: one-way canopies on rows 4 to 9 over bottomless gaps, caterpillars and tree frogs on them, birds on a path, three burr trees, and the goal pole on a great tree's bough with a hollow in its trunk to walk into. Its own tileset (285 tiles: 97 for the playfield, 188 for a parallax layer of clouds over the forest's crowns far below), sprite group (caterpillar, tree frog, bird and burr, four palettes after the blocks') and a tune in 6/8. Every required jump is at most 3 metatiles up and 48 pixels across, and at least 33 pixels narrower than the widest gap a walking jump clears at that height. What it used and exposed:

- **A perfect bounce: worked as designed.** Burrs are map bodies with `body_bounce` 255 and no `body_max_fall` (a limit would take height away, as `physics.h` says): each comes back up to the pixel row it dropped from on every landing (150 bounces logged in a run, every one peaking on the same row: 54 pixels above the canopy for the first and third trees, 22 for the second), rolling left until it runs out of canopy and falls into the gap.
- **A stage hook can steer the frame's objects.** The update hook runs after `objects_update()` has set velocities, so the treetops turns walkers and the fish back over bottomless drops, and stops frogs hopping into them, without touching `objects.c`: `map_collision_at()` down the column ahead finds a floor, or none. (A brick row over a canopy has a floor below it, so things step off it as in the other stages.)
- **An entity without a sprite** is a fine spawner: a burr tree is `entity_create(C_POS | C_STAGE(1))` with a timer; nothing draws it, and the frame removes it once it is far behind the camera, like any entity with a position.
- **Paths, again without gravity:** birds fly a looping weave (three steps whose turns cancel), and stomping one stops its path (`enemy_squash` calls `path_stop()`).
- **Compound meter:** `PsgSong.ticks_per_beat` 3 makes a tick an eighth note of 6/8, a beat a dotted quarter.
- **No new engine gaps.** Its tileset repeats the shared block tiles once more (tileset groups, above).

#### Stage 1-4, the castle

Grey stone halls over lava, with fire bars, leaping embers, salamanders and one-way stone ledges, a dragon on a bridge at the end, and after it the ending (`stage_castle.c`, `boss.c`). What it used and exposed:

- **A boss as a metasprite: worked.** The dragon is one entity and one map body (28x36) drawn as a 48x48 metasprite of nine 16x16 pieces, flipped as a whole to face the serval and drawn behind the playfield (`SPRITE_BEHIND_PLAYFIELD`) as it sinks, so the lava hides it. Its eight frames are cut into a 3x3 grid and share the parts that don't change (50 parts, 200 tiles of sprite VRAM): a job for an editor's build, done here by the example's conversion script. A metasprite has the same number of pieces in every frame, so a frame with an empty cell would still spend a hardware sprite on it (the dragon has none).
- **Palette effects still waited on palette writes** (implemented since; the example keeps its workarounds). The dragon's white flash, when the lever is pulled, is its whole palette mixed toward white with `color_mix()` when the stage loads, into RAM its sprite group points at, and switched per draw with `SPRITE_PALETTE`. The lava's glow is the backdrop's (`screen_set_backdrop()`), seen through the far colonnade's arches, whose palette is the stone's dimmed toward it at load. Lava that glows on its own tiles, or torchlight flickering on the walls, needs `tileset_set_colors()` (planned).
- **Hazards are per metatile.** A pit's lava is open metatiles tagged `TAG_HAZARD`, which the serval sinks into, over solid ones tagged too, on which embers (map bodies) rest out of sight. Its surface can't be tagged: the hazard test (`map_tags_in()` around the serval) includes the row under its feet, so a tagged row level with the floor would hurt a serval standing on the pit's edge. The surface is a picture half a metatile lower, and the hazard is the row below it.
- **A bridge falling with `map_set_cell()`: worked.** It takes 12 of `MAP_MAX_CHANGES` (64); with every block, brick and gem of the stage changed too, the stage stays under 30.
- **`camera_stop_x` only stops the camera.** The frame's camera follows the serval and never leads, so the arena holds once the serval is `CAMERA_LEAD` pixels from the screen's left edge: the bridge reaches back past that point, and the lever stands at the screen's right edge. Not an engine gap; a game wanting the arena framed before the fight would move the camera itself.
- **Fire bars need no entities.** Their balls are placed with `fx_cos()` and `fx_sin()`, drawn with `sprite_draw()` in the stage's draw hook and tested against the serval by hand; the dragon aims its fireballs with `angle_of()`.
- **The ending is two map layers** loaded on the black screen (background 3 the sky, background 2 the ground and the den), with the serval drawn between them: behind the near layer, the den's dark opening hides it, with no masking in code. Sprites drawn by hand disappear the moment the game stops drawing them, as it does during the fade back to the title, so START ends the ending only once the serval is inside its den.

## What `breakout` exposed

Written as a game developer would; the workarounds are in the example, with comments. Bricks are entities rather than map cells ([`play.c`](../examples/breakout/play.c) explains the choice): a map would have given square 16x16 bricks, hit the 64-cell limit of `map_set_cell` (`MAP_MAX_CHANGES`) on the first level's 84 bricks, slowed the ball at every contact (`body_bounce` was at most 255/256 for map bodies; 255 is now a perfect bounce, which keeps the whole speed off walls and ceilings) and reported the side touched (`body_contact`) without the cell; in exchange bricks would have cost no sprites or entities, and map bodies' 7-pixel steps would rule out tunnelling, which the entity version gets from a speed limit (3.5 pixels per frame against 8-pixel bricks and a 6-pixel ball).

- **`body_hit_side` worked as designed**, including against a moving paddle (it has `C_VEL`, so its motion counts). It judges two bodies alone, though: in a wall of bricks a ball grazing the corner of a shared face gets that face's side, so the game keeps a grid of brick slots and ignores faces with a neighbour behind them. `BODY_SIDE_INSIDE` never comes up in play, because the game puts the ball against the face it hit; it is handled defensively. The paddle uses Pong's generous rule: a ball clipping an end within 4 pixels of the top still bounces up.
- **`ecs_count`** is what the game asks for bricks left (`C_BREAKABLE`), balls in play and capsules falling. ~~Free slots stood in for missing API~~ (`MAX_ENT - ecs_count(0)` as the reserve kept for bricks, balls and capsules): closed by `ecs_free_count()`, which the game checks before creating optional effects.
- ~~**Gravity is global.**~~ Capsules should fall and balls fly straight, so the game accelerated the capsules itself. Closed by `body_gravity`: the game's gravity pulls the capsules (now `C_BODY`, leaving through the open bottom with `BODY_CONTACT_EXIT`), and the balls have `BODY_GRAVITY(0)`. The game's own capsule acceleration is gone. ~~`body_max_fall` is whole pixels per frame, so the capsules' top speed went from 1.5 to 2~~: closed by a fixed-point `body_max_fall`, and the capsules fall at 1.5 again.
- ~~**`sys_physics` doesn't report bounces.**~~ The game compared velocity signs after `sys_physics` to mirror the ball's angle and play the wall sound. Closed by `physics_set_contacts(true)` and `body_contact`: the ball's angle is mirrored off the walls it touched (`BODY_SIDE_*`) and a lost ball is one with `BODY_CONTACT_EXIT`, instead of comparing velocity signs and positions.
- ~~**A sprite's palette is part of its asset.**~~ Eight brick colors and a white hit flash were nine sprite IDs on the same tiles, and the glowing CATCH paddle three more. Closed by `SPRITE_PALETTE(n)`: one brick sprite takes all eight colors and the white hit flash, and the CATCH paddle is the paddle pieces drawn with its glowing palette (11 sprite IDs and 70 tiles fewer).
- ~~**Draw order on a layer is slot order.**~~ Balls and capsules are created after the bricks, so `sys_render` drew them behind; they borrowed `SPRITE_ABOVE_FOREGROUND` to come in front, since `sys_render_by_depth` cost about 7,500 cycles for this screen (it always cleared and summed 256 buckets). Closed by a cheaper `sys_render_by_depth`: it skips the sort when depths are already in slot order, and otherwise counts into only as many buckets as the depths span. With four depths (bricks, capsules, paddle, balls) the game now uses it, about 4,100 cycles over `sys_render` for 88 sprites, and nothing borrows a layer.
- ~~**No sprite wider than 32 pixels in one row.**~~ The 48-pixel wide paddle was three 16x8 pieces drawn by hand with `sprite_draw` after `sys_render`. Closed by metasprites: the paddle is one entity drawing `SPR_PADDLE` or `SPR_PADDLE_WIDE` (two or three pieces; a metasprite has the same number of pieces in each frame, so two assets), its CATCH glow `SPRITE_PALETTE(PAL_CATCH)` in `spr_flags`, depth-sorted with everything else.
- Map layers as plain backgrounds were easy: a 15x10 frame on background 2 and a 1x1 `MAP_LAYER_WRAP` pattern on background 3, one per level style. ~~The text-highlight gap shows again~~ (closed: the new score is in `TEXT_HIGHLIGHT`, no blinking arrows), and the game plays identically on the GBA and the web for the same input.

## What `shmup` exposed

Written as a game developer would; the workarounds were in the example, with comments, and most are now closed by engine API that replaced them. The playfield is the left 176 pixels and a HUD panel the right 64 ([`game.h`](../examples/shmup/game.h) explains why), the stage a map 426 metatiles tall that the camera climbs a pixel per frame, and the entity budget (120 entities and 123 sprites at most) is spelled out in `game.h`. Peak CPU on the GBA in the boss fight (40-odd bullets, the boss, shots, the debug readout on; a scripted run with the ship made invulnerable, RelWithDebInfo build): 33%, down from 44% while the camera still jumped back (its jump frames were the busiest, 37-44%; other frames peaked at 34%).

- ~~**Entities can't stay put on the screen while the camera moves.**~~ Closed by `SPRITE_SCREEN`: everything that flies has it and lives in screen coordinates, so the game no longer moves every flying entity by the camera's step each frame. Only the turrets are in the world, scrolling with the map. ~~Shots tested them with the camera added to the turret's position by hand, since `body_overlap` compared positions as they are~~: closed, `body_overlap` and `body_hit_side` add the camera for a screen-space entity against a world one, and the game's `shot_hits` is gone.
- **Rotating turrets** (the roadmap's rotation showcase). The fort cannons are 32x32 heads, each with its own `spr_angle` (a fort has two, and forts are far apart: two on screen at most, so two of the 32 matrices), turned toward the ship by at most 1.5 degrees a frame: the angle difference as an `s16` is the shorter way round, so the turn needs no wrap-around case. Shots leave from the muzzles (`fx_cos`/`fx_sin` of the angle, offset to each barrel), and the burst waits until the barrels are within 8 degrees of the ship. Two things were missing: ~~sprite scaling~~ (closed by `sprite_draw_ex` and `SPRITE_SCALED` with `spr_scale`: a destroyed cannon's head spins away shrinking, in sixteenths so its sizes share matrices) and ~~a way to see the sprite limits~~ (closed by `sprite_stats()`, in the debug readout). ~~The rotation pivot is the sprite's center~~, so the barrels of a 32x32 head reached only 15 pixels; a longer gun needed a 64x64 sprite (twice the scanline cost) or a pivot offset. Closed by metasprites, whose pieces turn about the metasprite's pivot: the head is a 32x32 dome on the pivot and 32x16 barrels 24 pixels ahead, so the muzzles are 39 pixels out. The pivot is the dome's center through the metasprite's origin (-10, -10: the middle of the entity's 20x20 hitbox), as a sprite's origin centers its art. Two hardware sprites per cannon (one more each, 125 of 128 at most) and 276 cycles of a scanline; the debug readout counts what a line loses (`sprite_stats_scanlines`). The emplacement and its crater are map art: four metatiles each, one 16x16 quarter flipped four ways (`MAP_SE_FLIP_*`), 8 tiles instead of 32.
- ~~**No fixed background layer.**~~ Closed by `MAP_LAYER_FIXED`: the HUD panel on background 1 used to be a one-row `MAP_LAYER_WRAP` layer whose tiles were the same on every pixel row, so scrolling changed nothing on screen; now it simply doesn't scroll.
- ~~**The camera can't scroll on forever.**~~ Closed by `map_set_scroll`. For the boss, the top 672 pixels of the map were empty and the camera jumped back 512 pixels (with every entity) whenever it reached the top, redrawing all three layers' windows. Now the camera stops at the top of the stage and the starfield scrolls on by its own offset, which counts on: no trick, no empty map, no redraw spike.
- ~~**`ECS_FOR_EACH` costs a full pool scan.**~~ Each loop visits all 128 slots, about 4,300 cycles from game code in ROM however few entities match. The first version tested each shot against the enemies with an `ECS_FOR_EACH` per shot: 48% CPU with four entities on screen; one pass over the pool listing each kind brought that to 17%. Closed by `ecs_gather` (in IWRAM), which now makes the lists once per frame.
- ~~**No path or pattern helper.**~~ Closed by `path.h`: the example's own `path.c` (about 60 lines of turtle steering) is gone, and its wave table's lines, swoops, weaves, dives, hover-and-leave and zigzags are `PathStep` tables (`PATH_MIRROR_X` for formations from the other side, `frames` 0 for a last step that never ends).
- ~~**No `atan2`.**~~ Closed by `angle_of`, which replaced a 33-entry `atan` table and a division.
- ~~**A sprite's palette is part of its asset**~~ (as in `breakout`): white hit flashes and the boss's red last phase were twins of seven sprites, 179 tiles of art loaded as 367. Closed by `SPRITE_PALETTE`: the flashes, the red phase and the orange carrier are palettes of the group (8 sprite IDs and 188 tiles fewer).
- ~~**Text centers on the whole screen.**~~ Closed by `text_print_centered_in` and `text_clear_area`, which replaced the example's `field_print`.
- **Not gaps after all:** a hitbox separate from the sprite is `body_w`/`body_h` plus the sprite's origin (a 4x4 hitbox in the 16x16 ship, shown while focusing). The per-scanline sprite budget doesn't bite: an 8x8 bullet costs 8 of a line's 1,210 cycles, so all 48 enemy bullets on one line take 384; only rotated or wide sprites could run it out, and then the hardware drops the highest OAM entries, which `sys_render_by_depth` gives to the lowest depths (the boss and the turrets, then the player's own shots here; enemy bullets are drawn in front of everything). One rotation angle shared by every spinner takes one matrix. `psg_music_pause` and `psg_music_set_tempo` did their jobs; gunfire on the noise channel replaces the drums while A is held.
- **Same game on the GBA and the web, with two caveats.** Two `random_range()` calls in one argument list made explosions differ between the builds: C leaves argument order open and GCC and clang differ, so random calls go one per statement. And the game converts its ASCII art at boot and loads the stage in a frame that overruns, so mGBA misses more refreshes than the page counts frames: a button pressed at mGBA frame N goes to page frame N - 11 on the title, and page shot N matches mGBA frame N + 10 in the stage ([development.md](development.md#web-builds) has the usual shift). With that, frames match pixel for pixel. (~~`NULL` isn't available without a C header~~: `platform.h` provides it now.)

## What the high-score table exposed

Written in `asteroids` against `save.h` as a game developer would (`scores.c`): one `ScoreTable` struct in slot 0, `SCORES_VERSION` 1, the default table on `SAVE_EMPTY`, `SAVE_CORRUPT` or `SAVE_OTHER_VERSION`, one `save_write()` when initials are confirmed, `save_erase()` for the demo reset. The save API itself needed no workaround. What it ran into elsewhere:

- ~~**No text highlight.**~~ `text_set_color` recolors all text, so the new entry was marked with blinking `> <` around its row. Closed by text styles: the new entry is in `TEXT_HIGHLIGHT` (yellow), in `asteroids`, `shmup` and `breakout`.
- **No text-input or menu helper.** The initials entry (cycling letters, a cursor, held-button repeat) is about 60 lines of game code. ~~Held-button repeat~~ is closed by `button_repeat`, which replaced the game's own, and `button_repeat_reset()` when the screen opens, so UP still held from thrusting changes no letter until it is pressed again (it replaced a guard that ignored UP and DOWN until either was pressed on the screen, after which a direction held from the game repeated too).
- **Sound IDs are one table.** Splitting the game into files means the screen in `scores.c` reports what happened (`EntryEvent`) and `main.c` plays the sounds, since the sound IDs live in `main.c`'s table; fine, but a shared header of IDs is what a generated project would have.
- ~~**Strings in fixed-size `char` fields.**~~ Initials are `char[3]` without a terminator (GCC 15 warns about `"ABC"` initializing a `char[3]` under `-Wextra`, so the defaults are written as character lists), and were copied to a 4-byte buffer to print. Closed by `%.3s` in `text_format`.

## What `blackjack` exposed

Written as a game developer would, to see what a card game (and a poker roguelike in the style of the deck-builders that are popular now) looks like on the engine; the workarounds are in the example, with comments. A card is 32x48. 52 whole faces would take 39 KB of the 32 KB of sprite VRAM, so a card on screen is composed of shared pieces ([`art.c`](../examples/blackjack/art.c) explains the design): a 32x64 base (blank face, golden court face, back), an 8x16 rank-and-suit index top-left and the same flipped bottom-right, a 16x16 suit (32x32 for aces, an emblem for courts) in the middle, and a shadow: five hardware sprites per face-up card (the face is now one metasprite of four pieces, plus the shadow). All sprites together take 582 of the 1024 sprite tiles (966 before `SPRITE_PALETTE`).

- ~~**No metasprites, and rotating a composed sprite is manual.**~~ Closed by metasprites (`SPRITE_ASSET_METASPRITE`, `SpritePiece`): a face-up card is one `SpritePiece` frame the engine places and rotates about the card's center, so `draw_piece` (each offset turned with `fx_cos`/`fx_sin` by hand) is gone; a card still shares one matrix (two: flips are part of a matrix, and the bottom-right index is flipped). A face's pieces depend on its rank and suit, so the 52 faces are built at boot ([`art.c`](../examples/blackjack/art.c), `build_face_pieces`), twice over: a whole-draw `SPRITE_PALETTE` recolors every piece, so the winning hand's gold outline is a second set of frames whose base piece alone is in `PAL_GLOW` (about 4 KB of pieces in EWRAM, no VRAM). The narrow two-piece flip steps are one more metasprite with a frame per step. Draw order between cards (newest in front, moving cards above resting ones) is still a hand-made sort: the cards are drawn directly, not entities, so `sys_render_by_depth`, which now sorts metasprites as one, doesn't see them.
- ~~**No sprite scaling.**~~ (Closed in the engine by `sprite_draw_ex`, with scales per axis and negative scales mirroring; the example still uses its frames.) The flip is animation frames built at boot (the card squashed to 24, 14 and 4 pixels); no hardware sprite is 16x64 or 8x64, so the narrow steps are a 16x32 or 8x32 top over a 16x16 or 8x16 bottom. The indexes and middle can't squash with the base, so they vanish during the flip. A scale factor on `sprite_draw_rotated` (the affine matrix already allows it) would give flips, hover zoom and pop-in for free; this style leans on scaling more than on rotation.
- ~~**Palette variants copy tiles.**~~ `sprite_group_load` gives every sprite ID its own VRAM copy of its tiles, even when two sprites point at the same `.tiles` with different palettes: the first version (gold-outlined winning cards, shadows, greyed buttons, gold and red digits, four colors of banner letters, all as full variants) needed 1532 tiles, and variants covering only the frames they needed 966. Closed by `SPRITE_PALETTE`: one sprite ID per shape (7 fewer), the variants drawn with another palette of the group: 582 tiles.
- ~~**No way to compose tiles at runtime.**~~ (Closed in the engine by `sprite_set_tiles()`, [sprites.md](sprites.md#runtime-tiles), which writes a sprite frame's tiles in VBlank as `tileset_set_tiles` does for backgrounds; the example still uses its metasprites.) Each dealt card can be composed once into its own 24 tiles and drawn as one sprite: 40 cards in the tiles the composed pieces take now, and one OAM entry for each face instead of four pieces.
- **Rotated sprites cost the scanline budget.** Rotation always uses the double-size box at twice the cycles per pixel: a tilted card costs about 330 of a scanline's 1,210 sprite cycles, so the table tilts at most three cards at once (the rest are drawn straight) and banner letters rotate only while they bounce in. ~~Nothing reports dropped sprites~~ (closed by `sprite_stats()` with `sprite_stats_scanlines(true)`: sprites a line lost, and the busiest line's demand); a plain (non-double) affine mode for small angles would still help.
- **No tween, easing or spring helpers.** Deals overshoot (ease-out-back), discards ease in, tilt is a damped spring, letters bounce, chips arc, numbers count: about 100 lines in `table.c` that every card game would rewrite.
- **No palette writes, no blending** (both are implemented since; the example keeps its swirl). The swirling background is two `MAP_LAYER_WRAP` layers computed from sines at boot, drifting by their own `map_set_scroll` offsets; a swirl in this style is usually palette cycling, which needs palette writes (nor could a shimmering "foil" card be done). Shadows are solid and the veil over the paint is a checkerboard, since there was no alpha blending. Palette writes are implemented since (`sprite_set_colors()`, `tileset_set_colors()`); `color_mix()` computes the colors for cycles and fades. Blending is implemented since (`screen_set_blend()`, with `SPRITE_BLEND` for the shadows; it pauses while `screen_set_brightness` fades); blackjack doesn't use it yet.
- ~~**Every map layer moves with the camera**~~ (as in `shmup`). Closed by `MAP_LAYER_FIXED` and `map_set_scroll`: both swirl layers are fixed and drift by their own offsets. That also fixed a bug: the drift went through `camera_set`, which clamps the camera to 0 when the layer on background 2 is smaller than the screen, so the background never moved.
- **Text is one 8x8 font, above every sprite but `SPRITE_ABOVE_HUD`, on an 8-pixel grid.** Totals, the bankroll, the bet and the pops are sprite digits (`draw_number`), so they can bounce and recolor; banners are 16x16 sprite letters. A large or variable-width font for sprites would replace both.
- ~~**Held-button repeat again**~~ (the bet): closed by `button_repeat` (with `button_repeat_set(16, 4)`, the game's old timing), and `button_repeat_reset()` when the bet opens, so a direction still held from the round or the title doesn't step it.
- **Fine as is:** `save.h` (the bankroll and stats, one slot, written after each round), `random_entropy` (a 312-card shoe shuffled with Fisher-Yates; the same input deals the same cards on the GBA and the web, pixel for pixel), priorities for effects over the music, entities for sparks and confetti.

**What a poker roguelike would need on top.** A hand of eight cards, five played, five jokers and a few consumables is 20-odd cards on screen: at five hardware sprites each (metasprites draw a card as one, but its pieces still cost four OAM entries, plus the shadow) that is most of OAM, so runtime composition of card faces (each card one sprite with its own tiles, with editions and stickers baked in: `sprite_set_tiles()`, implemented) comes first. Then scaling (hover and selection zoom, the flip, pops), per-draw palettes or palette writes (card editions, a cycling background), a variable-width font for joker descriptions and big score numbers, a tween library, and a cursor with held-button repeat for picking cards. The run state (deck, jokers, money, seed) fits a 2000-byte save slot, and seeded runs already replay identically.

## What `fireflies` exposed

Written first as Studio Advance's script compiler would emit it, by hand: [`fireflies.svm`](../tests/svm/fireflies.svm) was the listing (one op per line, the stack in a comment; now a test of the assembler, since the game is [written in Lua](#porting-fireflies-to-lua)), assembled at build time by [`tools/svm.py`](vm.md#tools) through `serval_add_script` (it began as a C file of assembler macros, `script.c`, that an example-local `asm.c` turned into the blob at boot; the tool replaced both and produces the same bytes). The whole game is a 1,132-byte blob: six objects (two of them threads), ten handlers and two subroutines: 421 ops in 800 bytes of code, then 88 bytes of strings (1,117 bytes and 416 ops before the VM revision: the handlers with locals gained an `ENTER`, `text_print_number` a width, and `INTERRUPTIBLE` went). The C ([`main.c`](../examples/fireflies/main.c)) is the frame loop in `vm.h`'s order, six lines of collision pairs (`ecs_gather` of two game components the objects' masks carry, `body_overlap`, `vm_event(firefly, player, VM_EV_COLLISION)`; since replaced by one `vm_collide(C_PLAYER, C_FIREFLY)` at boot), and a restart when the Room sets `G_RESTART` (`ecs_reset()`, `vm_load()`, two `vm_start()`s). A full round, TIME UP and a restart run with no VM warning, on the GBA and the web.

- ~~**A script can't size a body.**~~ An entity a script spawns gets the object's components but a 0x0 body, and `body_overlap` needs a size. Closed by `VM_P_BODY_W` and `VM_P_BODY_H` (properties 10 and 11, `C_BODY`): the Create handlers size the serval (24x20) and the fireflies (8x8).
- ~~**No numbers on screen.**~~ Scripts hold no strings, only string-table indices, so a score or a timer couldn't be shown. Closed by `VM_SYS_TEXT_PRINT_NUMBER` (SYS 12). It didn't blank what was there, so a number that shrinks (the timer's 10, then 9) or a score reset from 23 to 0 left digits behind, and the scripts print a string of spaces first (a string per width, four more ops per print); the VM revision gave it a width, right-aligned with blanks in front. The listing passes a width of 0 (just the digits), so its screens stayed the same.
- ~~**A waiting script can't react to an event.**~~ The settled design gave the firefly its whole life in its Create handler (`PATH_START`, `WAIT_MOVE`, `WAIT`, in a loop) and a Collision handler for the catch. With one script per entity, the loop is always waiting, so every Collision was dropped with a warning (seen in mGBA's log): nothing could be caught. First closed by an `INTERRUPTIBLE` opcode (0x33): the firefly opted in, and a Collision that arrived while it waited halted the loop and ran instead, for good. The VM revision replaced it with the general answer ([behaviours and reactions](vm.md#behaviours-and-reactions)): Create is the firefly's *behaviour*, which may wait, and Collision a *reaction*, which runs to its end on top of the waiting behaviour, on the same context; the behaviour then carries on waiting exactly as before. Here the catch `KILL`s the firefly, so its Destroy reaction runs and the behaviour is halted, as before; an enemy that patrols with waits and must react to a hit now simply goes on patrolling. `INTERRUPTIBLE` is gone and 0x33 unassigned.
- ~~**No per-instance variables.**~~ State that outlived a handler could live only in that context's locals (gone when the handler ended) or in globals, which are shared. The firefly's lifetime is a local of its loop, which works because a catch ends the firefly; an enemy that should go back to its patrol with its state, or any per-entity counter a Step handler keeps, had nowhere to go. Closed by instance fields: `VM_P_FIELD0` to `VM_P_FIELD0 + 15`, cells of each attached instance on the property page.
- ~~**No way to reach every instance.**~~ When time is up the Room can't tell the fireflies to fade, or count them; each firefly checks `G_PLAYING` at the top of its loop, so they fade within about two seconds of TIME UP instead of at once, and the live count is kept by hand in Create and Destroy. Closed by `NEXTI`, a loop over an object's instances (the listing still works the old way).
- ~~**No arrays.**~~ `LDG`/`STG` take a constant index, so a table (eight fireflies' handles, an inventory, a high-score list) couldn't be indexed by a computed value. Closed by `LDA`/`STA`/`LEN` and the blob's array table: RAM arrays in a pool of 1,024 cells, and constant ROM arrays.
- ~~**Scripts can't change components.**~~ After TIME UP the serval must stop catching, but the C glue finds players by their `C_PLAYER` bit, which a script couldn't clear. The Room swaps the serval for another object (`KILL` it, `SPAWN` a sitting serval without `C_PLAYER` in its place, copying its position and flip), which also gave the ending its look. Closed by `VM_P_TAGS`, the game component bits as a property.
- **Small ones.** ~~Switching sprites from a script can't restart the animation's timer~~ (closed by `VM_P_ANIM_TIME` and `VM_P_ANIM_STEP`), so a new animation's first frame can show a few frames short. `path_start` can't aim a path (no heading argument or property), so variety comes from three paths times four mirrorings; ~~a script can't stop a path~~ (closed by `path_stop`, SYS 13), so the paths end in a glide instead. Pausing the game would need C glue, since the C systems (paths, movement, animation) run whatever the scripts do.
- **Fine as is:** the two phases, with collisions reported between movement and `vm_events` so a catch is handled the frame it happens; `SPAWN` running Create in the same phase and `KILL` running Destroy in it, which kept the live count exact; `WAIT_MOVE` on paths, `WAIT_ANIM` on a one-shot fade, Animation End removing a sparkle; two threads started by C (scripts can't `vm_start`); `vm_bind` for the song and the paths; a global as the scripts' request to C; `vm_load` as a restart. The budget never came close: 167 ops in the busiest frame, across all scripts, against 256 per script per phase.
- **Costs** (the first measured script workload; details in [vm.md](vm.md#implementation-notes)). With 8 fireflies and the serval, in a Release build: the VM takes about 21,500 cycles a frame on average (peak 43,500), the whole frame 55,400 (peak 77,100, 27%). Ops cost about 195 cycles each, SYS work included, and most of a typical frame's VM time doesn't depend on them: about 14,500 cycles, the scheduler's passes over the 32 contexts and 128 bindings and the resume checks of the waiting scripts (about 500 cycles each). That was milestone 8's starting point (the script benchmark): a list of attached entities instead of scanning every slot, then the IWRAM question. Both are done (2026-10-10): the interpreter stays in ROM ([vm.md](vm.md#where-the-interpreter-runs)), and the scheduler visits only the contexts in use and the bound entities, so a round without input now takes 16,205 VM cycles a frame on average, 7,123 of them independent of the ops (23,022 and 13,620 before, timed by `fireflies_bench`, [development.md](development.md#script-benchmark)).

**What scripting a real game needed next** was per-instance variables, iteration over an object's instances, tables, game components a script can set and number formatting: the VM revision (milestone 6) added them all, for the [Lua subset](lua.md) to compile to. The engine-side collision pass over component pairs came with the API freeze: `vm_collide()` ([vm.md](vm.md#collisions)) replaced this example's C glue. ~~Still to come: a cheaper scheduler~~ (done with milestone 8's script benchmark, above). Writing the listing by hand also showed what the compiler must take care of: stack bookkeeping (the comment on every op), and ~~labels and forward references, the push width for constants (`BUTTON_DOWN` is 0x80, a `PUSH16`), and keeping string bytes after the code~~ (the assembler's job now: [`tools/svm.py`](vm.md#tools)).

## Porting `fireflies` to Lua

The game is now [`fireflies.lua`](../examples/fireflies/fireflies.lua), in the [Lua subset](lua.md), built by `serval_add_script()` with [`tools/svlua.py`](../tools/svlua.py) and `svm.py` (milestone 7 of [vm.md](vm.md#milestones)). The port keeps the listing's six objects, eight globals and component tags in the same order and draws the random numbers in the same order, so `main.c` didn't change (it did later, only to replace its collision glue with `vm_collide()`) and a round plays out frame for frame as the listing's did: in the web build, every frame captured from both (the acceptance script's seven, then every fifth frame of the first 1,300, every second one of a walk left and right with three catches, every fourth of the end of the round, the fade out and the restart; about a thousand) is pixel-identical. A full round with input on the web and one without on the GBA log no warning.

- **Size.** 1,201 bytes and 451 ops against the listing's 1,132 and 421 (+6%). The difference is the compiler being literal where the hand was clever: a numeric `for` keeps its counter and its limit in two frame slots and tests `i == limit` at the bottom, where the listing counted one local down to zero; `if not playing then break end` is a `JNZ` over a `JMP` where the listing wrote one `JZ`, and a `break` out of the spawner's inner loop lands on the outer loop's `JMP` (a peephole pass would thread both); the spawner's `dx` and `dy` are frame locals stored and read twice where the listing squared them with `DUP`. None of it matters at this size.
- **What the subset made easy.** Waits read as straight-line code (`wait_move()`, `wait(random_range(10, 40))`), a catch is `function Firefly:collision(serval)`, fields and properties are `self.x` and `serval.flags`, and the stack comments that every listing line needed are gone. Types caught what the listing left to care: fixed positions mixed with pixel counts (`self.x - 4` is four pixels) and `math.floor(player.x)` for the spawner's distance check.
- **Kept on purpose.** The score and the time are still printed as a blank string then the number without a width, as the listing did, to keep its screens; `text_print_number(col, row, n, width)` right-aligns, which would move the score. (Lua's `print` is not a builtin: it is a compile error naming `text_print` and `text_print_number`.) The Room still sets every global when a round starts, although `main.c` reloads the blob for each round, which sets every global to its [initial value](vm.md#array-table) (0 for all eight here): the same frames either way.
- **The listing lives on** as [`tests/svm/fireflies.svm`](../tests/svm/fireflies.svm), the assembler's largest fixture (`svm_test.py` assembles it with the game's headers and checks the round trip; `tests/svm/CMakeLists.txt` builds it through `serval_add_script`), and `svlua_test.py` checks that the Lua's tables still match it and runs the Lua on the VM: a whole round, a firefly's life, a catch, the walk.

## What `raycaster` exposed

`raycaster` ("Wisp Warren") was written to take the engine into 3D. Texture-mapped raycasters ran well on the GBA commercially, so the style had to be possible in Serval, and it wasn't: nothing in the API let a game set a pixel. The brief was to add as little as would do, and to keep the first-person logic in the game, because a rail shooter and a polygon demo are to follow and must not inherit a raycaster's assumptions.

**What the engine gained: the pixel surface** ([surface.md](surface.md), `surface.h`). Seven functions: on and off, the page to draw into, the flip, a clear, the 256 colors, and the hardware's affine transform of the picture. The transform turned out to be the part that matters most to a raycaster: the surface turned on its side makes a column of the view 160 consecutive bytes, and shown at half width it halves the columns, both for free. Turning the surface on costs the text layer, the map layers and half the sprite tiles (the bitmap pages are their video memory), which the engine arranges and enforces: text and map calls refuse, sprite groups load from tile 512.

**What the game had to bring itself,** each a candidate for the engine once a second 3D example shows its shape:

- **A finer sine.** `fx_sin()` and `fx_cos()` return 24.8 fixed point from 1,024 steps a turn: headings come in steps of 0.35° and a direction vector's length is off by up to 0.4%. At 30 pictures a second, turning 4.6° a picture, neither shows; a slower turn, or a floor drawn in perspective, would want 16 fraction bits. The game converts to 16.16 for everything after the lookup.
- **Division.** A raycaster divides by each ray's direction (twice) and by each wall's distance; the game has a 513-entry table of reciprocals with interpolation (`recip()` in `view.c`), accurate to 0.001%, about 40 cycles a call from IWRAM. `math.h` has nothing of the kind.
- **A fast copy.** The engine keeps libtonc's `memcpy32` to itself, and the compiler's own `memcpy()` and `memmove()` are in ROM as Thumb code. The compiler called them from the game's IWRAM code without being asked: to sort an array of structs, which cost about 150,000 cycles a picture until the game sorted indices instead, and then to shift the indices. The game's two word-copy loops are written through `volatile` pointers so that they stay loops. A public word copy and fill would have been the obvious tools.
- **Text over the surface.** The text layer is off, so every word the game shows is its own work: the HUD's digits and "PAUSED" are sprites of a 17-glyph font, and the title, menu and results (`title.c`) are lines composed at run time from two proportional fonts the game drew, into sprite frames 32 pixels wide, so a line costs a hardware sprite per 32 pixels whatever it says (one 8 x 8 sprite a character would have run out of the 128 on the list of controls alone). That is about 1,000 lines of the example. A sprite font in the engine, a call that composes a line into sprite tiles, or one that prints into the surface would serve every surface game.
- **Music with dynamics.** The tunes (`sound.c`) found the PSG music API's limits: a track has one volume, duty and envelope for all its notes, so accents and a drum kit with different envelopes are out; an effect (`PsgSound`) is one voice, so the end-of-run fanfares are single lines; a repeated note in an effect retriggers instead of holding. Per-note volume and duty in a track, and a non-looping song started as an effect, would cover them.
- **Timing a part of a frame.** `frame_cpu_cycles()` reports the whole previous frame. Finding where a picture's time went took a dozen builds with parts left out; a call that reads the cycle counter now would have shown it directly.
- **Pacing.** The picture takes two frames, split by hand at column 52 so that both fit; the split is a constant found by measuring. An engine can't know a game's drawing, but it could say how much of the frame is left.

**What it measured** (release build, `frame_cpu_permille()`, shown in the game with SELECT): each of a picture's two frames takes 55% to 80% of a frame's time, for 120 x 160 pixels of textured walls with things in view (64 x 64-texel wisps and props; textures and pictures fill 148 KB of EWRAM, converted from ASCII art at boot in about half a second). Drawing in a buffer in IWRAM and copying words to video memory costs 280 cycles a column and avoids video memory's 16-bit stores. Near walls and things are drawn two pixels a texel fetch (about 6 cycles a pixel against 11): they are the worst case, since they fill the screen, and the only one where it can't be seen. IWRAM: 15,988 bytes in release and 25,520 in a debug build.

**A caveat for games:** the engine's API is flat, so its prefixes are taken. The game's map functions were first called `map_cell()` and `map_cells`, which the compiler refused: `map_cell()` is the engine's. A game picks names outside the engine's prefixes (`grid_` here), as [lua.md](lua.md#reserved-names) asks of scripts.

## What `racer` exposed

`racer` ("Savanna Run") is the first game on the engine's raster effects, written to find out how far one effect at a time carries a game. A road is the hardest thing to ask of them: every screen line of it is drawn differently. It needed no new engine API. The road, the stripes and the sizes of things are the game's own arithmetic (`road.c`, `track.c`, portable and tested on the host, `tests/racer_tests.c`), and what it couldn't do it left out, which is this list.

**What carried it.** `raster_scroll()` on a road layer 16 metatiles (256 pixels) wide that wraps: the engine keeps all of such a layer in video memory, so every line may take any horizontal shift, and one still picture of a straight road becomes curves and steering. Palette writes (`tileset_set_colors()`, thirteen colours for each of the twelve road rows every frame) make the stripes, and a whole-layer scroll swings the sky. In the release build the road and the sky cost 18-19% of a frame at top speed in the sharpest curves; with the roadside objects, the traffic, the jeep and its dust, a scripted three-lap race peaks at 40.1% of a frame (median 32.9%), and at 49.5% (median 38.6%) with the SELECT readout's per-line count on (`sprite_stats_scanlines(true)`), which costs 5-6%. On the busiest frames of a scripted race the busiest scanline asked for 836 of its 1,210 cycles, no sprite was cut short or dropped, and at most 14 of the 32 matrices were used. IWRAM: 12,528 bytes in release.

**What one effect at a time ruled out:**

- **Hills.** A road that rises and falls picks its picture's lines vertically for the perspective while it shifts them sideways for the curves: two tables on one layer, a vertical and a horizontal one, and the engine runs one table on one axis of one background. The road is flat.
- **A raster sky.** `raster_backdrop()` would give the sky a smooth gradient for no tiles, but not while `raster_scroll()` runs. The sky is a tiled picture in one palette bank: nine bands, each blended into the next by a dither, the sun, hills and acacias in the same fifteen colours. The bump's shake moves whole layers down a line or two (`map_set_scroll()`), since a per-line shake would be a second table.

**The 256-pixel wrap bounds the road.** A line's road pushed more than 16 pixels past either side of the 240-pixel screen comes back in at the other, so every line's road must stay within 256 pixels. At most 160 pixels wide at the bottom, the road keeps 56 pixels of room each way there: it follows the car across the road only up to 0.7 of the car's 1.5 half-road widths, and the jeep sprite moves for the rest. The curves are bounded the same way, by a clamp on every line that the circuit's sharpest curve never reaches; a wider road would leave less for both. The shifts are whole pixels, so the outside edge of the sharpest curves steps by a few pixels near the bottom, where lines are widest.

**Stripes by palette are bounded by the banks.** A background tile has one palette bank of 15 colours, so the stripes are four materials (grass, rumble strip, tarmac, dash) times three groups of lines in each 8-line tile row, 12 colours, plus the edge line's: a band of stripes can't be finer than a group of two or three lines, and every road row takes a bank of its own (12 of the 15; the sky has one, two are spare). Far up the road, where a light and a dark stretch would fall within a few lines, the groups would alias, so the top 40 lines of the road (rows 0-4) show the mix of the two, a haze, and the stripes begin 40 lines below the horizon.

**No live pitch on the tone generators.** `psg_play()` takes only a sound's number, and nothing changes a playing tone's pitch. The engine's drone is 16 sounds from 50 to 168 Hz, each held until replaced, and a new one plays only when the speed reaches another step: a rise in steps, not a glide. Nothing stops one channel either: the drone is silenced by playing a quiet wave note that fades within a frame, since `psg_stop_all()` would also end a paused tune's pause. And `psg_music_pause()` pauses only the music: an effect started just before the pause sounds on into it.

**Shrunk sprites cost their whole box.** A sprite only shrunk (`sprite_draw_ex()`, both scales within `FX_ONE`) is drawn in its own box at half the cost of a grown or turned one, but it costs that box on every line it covers however small it looks: a 64x64 acacia shrunk to 8 pixels would still cost 64 lines of a 64-pixel box. So every roadside object and vehicle is drawn at boot in four sizes, each half the last, and a draw picks the size that keeps it between half and all of its box (398 sprite tiles for the four sizes of nine objects, of the game's 806). The scales come in sixteenths, so the draws at one scale and flip share a matrix: 16 matrices at most, however many objects are in sight. The hardware has no 16x64 sprite, so a checkpoint's post is two 16x32 sprites, one lifted on the other.

**A first message is slow.** A text style builds its glyphs the first time it is used, tens of thousands of cycles: the first message of a race took 70% of a frame. The game uses each style once at boot, under the black screen.

## What `rhythm` exposed

`rhythm` ("Beat Paws") is the first game timed by its music, written to find out what a game needs to keep time with tracker music. A rhythm game is the strictest test: a note drawn or judged a few hundredths of a second off is felt, and a song lasts over a minute. The engine could play a module, pause it and change its speed, but not say where it was.

**What the engine gained: the music's position** ([audio.md](audio.md#position)). `music_position()` and `music_ticks()`: the module's ticks heard since row 0 at the start of the picture being drawn, in 24.8 fixed point, exact to the sample, never going back through loops and jumps, frozen while paused, with the tick's length in samples (`tick_samples`, `MUSIC_RATE` a second) to turn the game's own times into ticks; for scripts, SYS call 53. `music_play()` now starts row 0 at the first sample of the next mix. The charts are in the same ticks, written with the songs from one source by [`make-example-audio.py`](../tools/make-example-audio.py) (its `rhythm` set, checked by `rhythm_charts_test.py`), so the game never converts a time into a frame count. Counting frames since `music_play()` would have drifted three ways:

- **Ticks are a little short.** Maxmod makes a tick a whole, even number of samples: 394 at calm's 100 BPM (0.05% fast), 316 at stride's 124 (0.60%), 266 at rush's 148 (0.13%). Notes timed from the nominal tempo would be 37, 394 and 107 ms late at each song's end: stride's past the GOOD window (80 ms) after about 13 seconds.
- **The start varied.** Before, row 0 sounded 0 to 1 tick into the first mix, by what had played before (a whole tick at boot). The deterministic start moved the `jukebox`'s first note 19.9 ms sooner, exactly the tick it used to lose.
- **Frames that overrun.** The VBlank handler mixes a frame of music that `frame_count()` doesn't count. This game never overran (its busiest frame took about 27% of a frame in a RelWithDebInfo build with the scanline count on), so this one stayed a reason, not a measurement.

**What it measured.** An autoplay (R on the song select) presses each note in the first frame whose press time has reached it and is judged at that press time like a player's: it hits every note of all six charts PERFECT (35 ms), through the last, in mGBA and in the page, and through pauses and a retry (calm HARD, in mGBA). In mGBA, the sound of a whole song with the autoplay's blips less the same song with none (rush HARD, 353 notes on 268 ticks, 76 seconds from the first to the last) puts each blip 8.5 to 25.4 ms after its note's place in the music: a spread of one frame (16.9 ms), from whole-frame input. The first 20 notes average 16.3 ms and the last 20 17.9 ms, a trend of 0.6 ms a minute: no drift.

**Input is known to the frame.** `frame_begin()` polls the buttons once a frame, just after the VBlank before the one `music_ticks()` names, and a press came on average half a frame before the poll that saw it: the game judges a press at `music_ticks()` less 25 ms (1.5 frames), ±8.4 ms (half a frame) either way, a quarter of PERFECT's window on each side. Nothing finer exists in the API, and the web delivers input once a frame anyway. The calibration's median of 16 taps keeps single taps' half frames out of the offset, but every press is still judged to within a frame.

**The web's latency belongs to the player's calibration.** The page's sound comes through an audio queue that starts at 40 ms and is trimmed above 150 ms, then the device's own output latency, and the picture at the browser's next composite: nothing reports these to C ([open-questions.md](open-questions.md)). The game's calibration, a click at 120 BPM to tap along to (silent taps, so that only the click is heard), takes the median of 16 taps' errors, adjustable by a millisecond, up to ±200 ms (a latency beyond that is not covered), and saves it with the records; a positive offset moves the presses back, and the notes' drawing with them, so a note crosses its pad as a press there is PERFECT. Driven by the same buttons, the page measures what mGBA measures (−149 ms for a script that taps every 30 frames against a beat of 29.8): the arithmetic is the same, but no headless tool hears a device's latency, so the web's real offset is the player's to find.

**A pause freezes a frame later.** `music_pause()` stops the position where the music stops being heard: after the frame of music already mixed, one frame after the call. The game reads the position on every paused frame, so the notes move one more frame's worth (2 pixels) after the pause and then stand; CONTINUE resumes with no jump.

**The end comes a frame after `music_playing()`.** Maxmod stops a module played once as it processes its last tick, before mixing it, so a song ends at its nominal length, which `music_ticks()` reaches a frame after `music_playing()` turns false. The game ends a song when every note is judged and the ticks reach the module's length (from the generated header), with a safety net: every note left is a MISS if the music stays stopped for 30 frames. Each song ends on a held note that fades through its last bar, so the silent tick isn't heard.

**Headroom for the tone generators.** The PSG plays at full volume beside Maxmod's mixer, and the web maps the mixer's 8-bit sum to full scale and clamps it. With the songs at 0.7 of full volume the music peaked at −2.5 dBFS in the page and −0.1 dBFS with the blips on top, so the songs' generator scales every instrument and volume by 0.56 (`MIX_GAIN`): with blips, −1.0 dBFS in the page and −2.6 in mGBA (−6.5 for the music alone), nothing clipped. (The `jukebox`'s theme, mixed louder, has 473 samples at full scale in a recording of its page.)

**Art drawn at boot pays per pixel.** The field, the stage and the sprites are drawn by code at boot, as `racer`'s and `servle`'s are. The first version asked a function for every pixel (is it in the spotlight, the grass, a paw, a pad's rim?), in Thumb code from ROM: 200-350 cycles a pixel, 7.4 million cycles for the sprites' outlines alone (nine neighbours tested for every pixel), 27.1 million in all: 146 frames (2.4 seconds) between the splash and the title in a release build. Painting a line at a time from per-line and per-column tables, drawing each shape once and looking it up, and outlining from the painted pixels took it to 9.6 million cycles and 32 frames, for the same picture.

**Smaller findings:**

- **Words in four pieces.** The hardware has no 64x16 sprite, so PERFECT (82 pixels, the 5x7 font doubled) is four 32x16 sprites.
- **A sprite behind a background can show through one in front of it.** The panel the notes come out from under is a background in front of the sprites, and the progress marker a sprite in front of it. The hardware takes a pixel's color from the first sprite drawn there but its layer from the frontmost sprite's, so a note hidden under the panel showed around the marker; the game holds a note back until it is clear of the marker.
- **Two examples, one test binary.** The host tests link every example's portable files together, so the game's `records_update()` collided with `racer`'s and became `records_add()`.

**What it costs.** At most 18 of the 128 hardware sprites, and its busiest scanline 176 of the 1,210 cycles, in a HARD song. IWRAM: 19,620 bytes in release (Maxmod's included) and 25,192 in a debug build; 79 KB of EWRAM (the art, its tables and the play state). The three songs, the title loop and the calibration's click are a 79 KB sound bank; the six charts, 1,108 notes, take about 9 KB of ROM.

## What `boardgames` showed

`boardgames` ("Paws & Kings") is chess and checkers in one ROM, written to find out what a game whose work is a search needs from the engine and from itself. It needed no engine API and changed nothing in `src/`, `include/` or `third_party/`: a search is plain C on fixed pools (the example's own `search.c`, `chess_*.c`, `checkers*.c`, portable and tested on the host, `tests/chess_tests.c`, `checkers_tests.c`, `boardgames_tests.c`), and what limited it was the CPU's time, which is this list.

**One search, two games.** `search.c` knows no game. It is iterative deepening with alpha-beta, quiescence, a 4,096-entry transposition table, killers and a stable move order, and each game gives it a `SearchGame` table of functions: generate, make, unmake, evaluate, key, draws, whether a side is in check, the game's ply for the opening rule. A node costs a few indirect calls (a load and a `BX`), which no measurement found: the cost was in generating moves and evaluating, ~6,600 cycles a node in both games, thousands of times a call's few cycles. Moves cross the table as `u32` (chess's `Move` is 16 bits, a checkers move is a jump path). Checkers' compulsory jumps and chess's check at the horizon are two flags and a callback (`noisy_forced`, `in_check`), not two searches.

**A search that can stop.** It has no recursion and no state in C locals. The tree is a stack of frames in EWRAM (64 plies: alpha, beta, the best move, the slice of the shared 4,096-move stack, the move being tried, a state: entered or generated), and `search_step(budget)` pauses only before it counts a node, a pure pre-check, so a search run in steps of 1, 3 or 37 does the same work in the same order as one run whole. Tests compare stepped runs with whole ones (move, score, depth, nodes), with a plain recursive negamax at a fixed depth, across the table's generation wrap and for a first iteration cut short by the node limit. The search owns 95 KB of EWRAM (the table 48 KB, the move stack 24 KB, a copy of the position 14 KB); all of the game is 228 KB of EWRAM (256 KB there is) and no malloc. A deliberate weak move or a random choice among near-equal moves (CUB) uses the search's own seeded generator, consumed once when it finishes, so the same position, strength and seed give the same move on the GBA, on the web and in every build type.

**Cycles per node, measured on the GBA** (a bench ROM: a timer cascade around searches of four chess positions and two checkers positions, ROM code in Thumb as in the real build; cycles over nodes):

| | Chess | Checkers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First working version (`ee170c6`) | 13,710 | 6,746 |
| Quiescence stands pat before generating; evaluation kept incrementally (piece-square sums, phase and pawn words updated by make and unmake, a pawn-structure cache); the generator walks the occupancy's set bits; only moves to or between the checker's squares when in check | 6,583 (-52%) | 6,773 (unchanged) |
| Nodes a second (a busy thinking frame, release) | about 410, then 1,123-1,494 | about 770-1,612 |

The same search on a PC (gcc -O2, x86-64) does 1.8-3.5 million nodes a second, and the work to get the GBA's number was mostly not computing: not generating moves before the evaluation says they are needed, and not rescanning the board for what a move changes. The first working version's profile (generate 10.7k, captures 5.8k, evaluate 4k cycles) is why those, and not the indirect calls, were the targets.

**The allowance follows the frame.** A fixed number of nodes a frame has to be sized for the dearest position, so most frames wasted the CPU or the busiest frame ran over (at 8 nodes a frame the chess peak was 87%, at 10 about 100%). Nodes also differ in cost by 19% of a frame between equal counts, so the search counts work units (16 a node entered, 16 a move list plus 1 a move) and `search_pace()` turns the last thinking frame's `frame_cpu_cycles()` over the work it did into cycles a unit: a higher cost is believed at once, a lower one a sixteenth at a time. The allowance is the budget, `frame_budget_cycles()`, times 0.76 over that estimate (0.8 reached 95.5% on the busiest frame): two divisions a frame, none per node. A frame that did less than a quarter of the work it was given (it started or ended a search, or the game was busy) teaches nothing, and one where a piece slides gets half. Thinking frames average 66-71% of a frame and the busiest was 89%. None of this can change a result: the allowance only decides where a step stops, so the web, whose `frame_cpu_cycles()` is the page's own, gives each game a fixed allowance (`SearchGame.frame_work`: chess 630 units, checkers 890, the GBA release averages) and plays the same moves, a little more slowly or quickly.

**The levels**, measured on the GBA (games played on, the computer moving for both sides, from chess's Kiwipete position and a checkers middlegame):

| | Depth, nodes (`SearchLevel`) | Think time, average / longest (release; `gba-ci`) | Depth reached |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chess CUB | 2, 1,500 (a random move within 60, a blunder in 150 of 1,000) | 0.6 / 1.4 s (0.7 / 1.6) | 0-2 |
| Chess HUNTER | 4, 3,600 | 2.6 / 3.0 s (2.7 / 3.1) | 1-3 |
| Chess SERVAL | 8, 10,000 (a random opening within 15 of the best for 8 plies) | 6.7 / 7.4 s (7.2 / 7.9) | 2-4 |
| Checkers CUB | 3, 1,500 | 0.26 / 0.6 s (0.26 / 0.7) | 3 |
| Checkers HUNTER | 10, 4,000 | 2.4 / 2.95 s (2.6 / 3.1) | 5-10 |
| Checkers SERVAL | 14, 11,500 | 7.3 / 7.7 s (7.9 / 8.1) | 7-13 |

The limit is nodes, the think time follows from the rate, and the longest stays under 8.2 s. Chess SERVAL is weak: chess's quiescence is heavy (Kiwipete's depth 1 alone is ~1,300 nodes), depth 3 from White there takes 17,000 nodes, about 11 s, out of reach of a wait a player would accept, so it plays depth 3 in busy middlegames and 4-5 in quiet ones. Checkers branches less and reaches 12-13. CUB, with its blunder chance, lost to HUNTER in scripted full games of both kinds (chess by checkmate at move 27, checkers at move 28), and took material when it was offered. `gba-debug` (-O0, nothing in IWRAM) is several times slower (checkers SERVAL's opening move took 30-50 s) and is not a way to play.

**IWRAM placement.** The generator, the attack test, the legality check, make and unmake, the evaluation and the move order are ARM code in IWRAM (`CHESS_HOT`, only in optimized builds: at -O0 it does not fit): generation 3x faster, evaluation 2.8x, 31,000 to 13,000 cycles a node before the algorithmic changes above. `generate()` is compiled at -O2 (a `#pragma GCC optimize`) because -O3 would have put IWRAM at 22,540 bytes, over the 22 KB ceiling; -O2 is 4 KB smaller for about 1% of speed. In the end the release build uses 21,096 bytes, 1,432 under the ceiling (the save's 848 bytes and the art's tables are in EWRAM). Kept out for want of room: `search_step()` in IWRAM would be 8% faster in chess and 7% in checkers for 3.3 KB; moving `score_moves` and `pick_next` gained 2% and was reverted. A debug build uses 18,636 bytes, 2,460 fewer than release, the one example whose IWRAM code is released only by optimizing ([development.md](development.md#memory-use)).

**The save's one-byte moves.** A game in progress is its moves, not its position: each is one byte, the move's index in the legal-move list of the position it was played from, up to 800 plies (`GAME_PLY_MAX`, a draw when reached), in one 848-byte versioned slot with the records (SRAM or Flash; EEPROM's slot is too small). CONTINUE replays the list through the rules, which also proves it: an index out of range, or a game over before the end, drops the save with "SAVED GAME LOST" and the records stay. The save is written once a move, after the search and the slide, in a frame of its own: about 153,000 cycles of the engine's `save_write()`, 89-93% of a frame.

**Smaller findings:**

- **Clearing a table is a frame.** One `memset` of the 48 KB table was a 105% frame; it is cleared in 16 steps, and only every 63rd search, each search having a generation that makes the older entries read as empty.
- **A first iteration may not finish.** With a node limit the first depth might not, so a search stopped inside it keeps the best of the root moves it has searched to the end (the one under way is left out), which bounds the think time at the price of a weaker first move at CUB and SERVAL's lowest depth.
- **Art at boot, again.** The board is a map of 16x16 metatiles with each tile kept once; the 16x16 pieces are drawn in two palettes and the logo is blown up from a 5x7 font; the dimmed look behind the menus is premixed palettes swapped by palette writes, the logo's colors left bright.
- **Two examples, one test binary.** The shared host test binary links each example's portable files, so this game's public names start with `board_records_`, `pos_`, `ck_`, `chess_`, `match_`, `search_` or `checkers_` (the portable files) and `view_`, `play_`, `screens_`, `sound_` or `art_` (the GBA side), plus `CK_` for checkers' constants.

**What an engine could offer later** (observations, not changes):

- **A mid-frame cycle query.** The allowance is the last frame's cost per unit of work, so it aims under the budget (76%, so that a dear frame does not drop) and never uses the rest. A call that said how many cycles the frame has used so far (`frame_cpu_cycles()` is the whole frame's, read at its end) would let a game stop a step at the budget instead of before it: the same frames could be filled further than the 70% they average (not measured). The web's page would answer it with its own clock and give the same moves.
- **IWRAM code that costs no space it doesn't use.** The hot loop wants to be in IWRAM and the game cannot afford all of it (`search_step()`: 3.3 KB for 8%). A way to load a function into IWRAM for a screen and drop it afterwards, an overlay, would let a game pay for it while it thinks only; the linker's `.iwram` is fixed at link time.
- **A cheaper save frame.** The save is 89-93% of a frame in one call; a write in steps over a few frames would cost nothing visible. It is a property of the save media on the GBA, so a stepped write would be only as good as the medium allows.

**What it costs.** IWRAM: 21,096 bytes in release and 18,636 in a debug build. 228 KB of EWRAM, 95 KB of it the search. A thinking frame averages about 70% of the CPU and the busiest 89%, by design.

## What `den` showed

`den` ("Serval Den") is a virtual pet that lives by the cartridge's clock, written as the showcase of [`rtc.h`](rtc.md) and to find out what a game that lives while it is off needs. It needed no engine API and changed nothing in `src/`, `include/` or `third_party/`. Its work is in the game's own rules, which is where a clock's trouble is.

**Time is the clock's seconds, and the core steps whole minutes.** The pet (`pet.c`, with `litter.c`, `album.c`, `den_save.c` and `events.c`) is portable C with no `rtc_*` call: it is given the time. A pet left for a day is the pet that lived that day, because boot catches the core up minute by minute (an exact, split-invariant loop: a catch-up in pieces ends where one run does; a dead pet is jumped over at once, so the loop stays under about 2,160 steps). The game's use of the clock is `clock.c`: the clock is read at boot and then at most once a second (a read costs 3-4 scanlines with interrupts off, [rtc.md](rtc.md#what-a-read-costs)), and the hour of day comes from `rtc_time_from_seconds()`.

**What the clock can do, and what the game does.** The owner's rules are plain; the player owns the clock, so each case has an answer:

| The clock | The game |
| --- | --- |
| Goes on normally | The time away is lived, then summed up on a card (AWAY 9H 30M, what happened, how it is now) |
| Goes backwards | No time has passed; every stored time moves back by the same amount, so age and neglect are kept |
| Was reset (`rtc_was_reset()`, a dead battery) | The time away is not counted and the card says so; a game-clock offset kept in the save makes the game's time resume where it was, so an old pet does not become young (a rebase would clamp its birth to 0), and the day and night shift by the lost time |
| Jumps more than 30 days ahead, at boot or while it runs (a web page's clock can) | The game asks (YES lives the days, which a pet does not survive; NO counts none) |
| Moves on more than an hour during play | The screen goes dark, the time is lived to the end and summed up once, as at boot, so that a grow-up scene never plays just before a death |
| Is missing | The no-clock screen, and nothing else: the game is nothing without it |

**The frame budget decides how a catch-up is shown, not its result.** A catch-up step of 15 minutes of life a frame costs 70% of a frame at worst, 30 minutes 88%, so the den catches up by 15 minutes a frame when the gap is short (up to an hour: the debug warp's) and a longer one is lived in black. The worst frame of each screen in `gba-release`, measured with temporary probes while the example was built (60 fps held, none over budget): the den in play 43-49% (a save written in the same frame included), a 25 hour catch-up 74%, growing up at boot 62%, a tombstone to a new kitten 60%, the litter 51% (84.7% before its save was moved out of the frame in which the scene is set up), a jump during play 41%, the album 18%, Acacia Climb 14%, Yarn Bat 12%, High Pounce 8%.

**Art generated ahead of time.** Drawing the den and the serval at boot took 1.9 seconds of title card. `art_draw.c` still draws them, by rule in C, but on the host: `art_gen` writes `art_data.c` (const data in ROM, as `servle`'s word tables are generated) and the CTest `den_art_data` fails when it is stale, which took the release boot, splash included, from 324 frames to 272 (the debug boot from 676 to 376) and left no drawing code in the ROM. The serval at three sizes, its coat as a palette (the spots are three sets of colours that a pattern shows or hides) and the sky's four times of day are sprites and palette writes: five more sprite palettes show a visitor and a litter in their own coats, and `sprite_draw_ex()` enlarges the growing serval, the stone and the album's portraits.

**Saves and generations.** The save (452 bytes, packed little-endian, with a version) holds the pet, the album, the games' best scores as the den's records (they go on from one generation to the next), the stage last shown (so a grow-up is shown once) and the time last seen. It is written every 5 minutes of play, after every care action, at every change of stage or mood and after every life event.

**What it would ask of the engine.** The PSG has no panning, so High Pounce's rustles are mono (loudness for distance, and an arrow over the serval's head for direction); a pan on the noise channel would give the game stereo. A debug build's check of upward slides on square 1 found the serval's mew (`SND_MEW`) silenced early, and the sound was lowered and slowed: the check did its job. `rtc_was_reset()` says the clock lost its power but not how long it was off, which the game cannot know (it uses the offset). Neither is a gap in the frozen API; the first is a candidate for a 1.x addition.
